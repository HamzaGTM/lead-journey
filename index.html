Follow one new lead through HubSpot. You will see every step it takes, how long each wait is, and when a message is allowed to go out. Some of these behaviours can look like mistakes from the outside. Each one is set up on purpose, and this page shows why.

Where it starts

A student asks for information

A student lands on one of our bootcamp pages and fills out the Request More Information form: full name, email address and mobile number.

By submitting, they agree to receive calls and texts about the program. HubSpot also records which program they asked about. That one detail decides which emails they get later.

Inside HubSpot

HubSpot marks them as a New lead

The form sends the student into HubSpot. A student we have never seen before gets the lead status New.

Lead status is the field that runs the whole system. Our reps change it as they work a lead, and some changes happen automatically. The automation watches it at every step.

Every New lead goes into the router, a short workflow that welcomes the student and then sends them to the right program.

One rule that applies everywhere

When a person takes over, the automation steps aside

If a lead's status changes to any of these, HubSpot removes them from every nurture workflow straight away. It does not matter where they are, even halfway through a wait.

A lifecycle stage of Opportunity or Customer removes them too. Tap a status to watch a lead leave the flow.

WhyOnce a rep is talking to a student, or the student has enrolled, automated follow ups would talk over them. So if a lead stops getting emails, check the lead status first. A change there is the usual reason.
Router · step 1 of 6

Set as a marketing contact

The first thing the router does is mark the student as a marketing contact. HubSpot only sends marketing emails to marketing contacts, so without this step none of the emails below could go out.

Router · step 2 of 6

Wait 5 minutes

The router pauses for five minutes before the first message. This wait is set on purpose.

If you test the form yourself, expect the first email a few minutes later, and only inside the send window further down this page.

Router · steps 3 and 4 of 6

A welcome email, then a welcome text

The student gets a thank you email, followed by a text message sent through Kixie, our calling and texting tool.

These two messages are the same for every student, whatever program they picked. Messages about their specific program start after the next step.

Router · step 5 of 6

Wait 1 business day

The router now waits one business day. Weekends do not count, so a student who arrives on a Friday moves on the following Monday.

Router · step 6 of 6

Sorted by program

HubSpot reads the program the student asked about and hands them to that program's own track. There are six:

If the program does not match any of the six, the student goes to the Cybersecurity track, so every lead still gets follow up. Kickstarter now runs inside the downsell campaign instead of having its own track.

Every program track

5 emails and 5 texts over about three weeks

Each program has its own copy, but every track keeps the same rhythm. Each touch is one email plus one text, spaced out in business days.

That is about 15 business days from the first program email to the last. The exit rule still applies at every point, so a lead who books a meeting leaves the track right away.

The send window

Messages only go out between 12 pm and 7 pm EDT

Every email and text in these workflows waits for this window. If a step comes due outside it, HubSpot holds that message and sends it when the window opens again.

When a lead comes back

A disqualified student who returns is marked Re-engaged

Some students tell us no, and a rep marks them Disqualified. The exit rule takes them out of the automation.

If that student fills out a form again later, HubSpot marks them Re-engaged automatically. A separate workflow picks them up and sends them straight into their program's track.

Speed to contact

How quickly do we reach a new lead?

Only two waits sit between a sign up and our messages, and we set both of them ourselves.

  1. 5 minutes after sign upBefore the welcome email and text. Whatever form they use, the student does not hear from us instantly.
  2. 1 business day after the welcomeBefore the student moves into their program track. This wait works well, and we keep it.

Nothing else in the router or the program tracks holds up the first message. So the quickest way to reach leads faster is to shorten that first 5 minute wait.

Welcome arrives about 5 minutes after sign upThis is how the router is set today.

It is one setting on one step of the router. The send window still applies, so a student who signs up outside 12 pm to 7 pm EDT gets the welcome when the window opens.
